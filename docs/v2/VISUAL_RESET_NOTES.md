# Bounce BTTS 2.0 — Visual Reset

Status: active V2 design direction

## Why this reset exists

The first Phase 2 preview proved the new shell and data wiring, but did not move far enough away from the 1.x visual structure. It remained recognisably card-led and therefore did not meet the intended premium product standard.

## V2 visual target

Bounce 2.0 should feel like **Edinburgh heritage × modern football broadcast × private members club**.

The redesign should be materially different from 1.x at first glance:

- open editorial composition rather than card grids;
- large confident typographic hierarchy;
- deep oxblood / maroon, near-black, warm stone and restrained gold;
- subtle St Giles, Heart of Midlothian mosaic, Edinburgh skyline and Tynecastle details;
- full-width visual bands and ruled ledgers instead of repeated rounded containers;
- cards reserved for genuine emphasis or exceptional states;
- calm, fast interaction and mobile-first scanability;
- premium does not mean decorative overload: imagery is atmospheric and hierarchy remains functional.

## Core pages establishing the language

### Dashboard

A matchday editorial surface built around the current gameweek lifecycle. The current action and selection dominate the top of the page; league pulse, everyone's picks and standing flow beneath as open ledgers and bands.

### Stat Centre

A data-room / sports editorial destination. It is structured into **League**, **Players**, **Form & Trends**, and **Records** rather than presenting a flat collection of stat cards. Statistics should tell a story and support drill-down.

### Admin

A league control centre. It presents real system state, the current gameweek, submissions, fixture coverage and attention items first. Routine operations are separated from advanced intervention and recovery tooling.

## Phase 3 acceptance

Phase 3 is accepted only when:

1. Dashboard, Stat Centre and Admin are visibly and structurally different from 1.x at first glance.
2. The dominant page language is editorial bands, ledgers, typography and spacing — not repeated rounded cards.
3. Existing league data and canonical rules remain authoritative; the visual reset does not introduce a parallel scoring, fixture or scheduling model.
4. Dashboard preserves current-gameweek context, current selection, member picks and league standing.
5. Stat Centre exposes League, Players, Form & Trends and Records as a coherent analytics flow.
6. Admin establishes a control-centre hierarchy with routine status and operations ahead of advanced tooling.
7. iPhone and iPad layouts remain readable without accidental horizontal page scrolling.
8. Edinburgh/Hearts heritage detail is subtle, atmospheric and consistently applied.
9. Preview builds cleanly and remains isolated from main/production.
10. Remaining V2 surfaces can inherit the same design language without reintroducing a separate card-first system.

## Review test

A V2 screenshot should not plausibly be mistaken for the 1.x interface. If the dominant impression is still a stack of rounded cards, the redesign has not gone far enough.

This direction applies to every later V2 surface so the final product remains stylistically consistent.
